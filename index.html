THE PROJECT AT A GLANCE

Better information.
Better possibilities.

Our research investigates an AI-enabled coconut land utilization, WCLWD severity assessment, germplasm identification and community intelligence advisory system. It addresses four information needs that are usually handled separately through manual observation and specialist judgement.

The Coconut Diagnostics Suite brings independently developed modules into a shared web interface. Each accepts its own input, uses its own dataset and returns its own result. Integration happens at the application level.

Research prototype: field, expert and external validation remain necessary before operational use. There is no combined system accuracy.

LITERATURE SURVEY

Building on established research

Transfer learning with EfficientNet and Prototypical Networks supports recognition with limited labelled images. YOLO-based aerial detection supports palm counting; vegetation indices and distance transforms add occupancy and free-space information.

WCLWD research motivates image classification and severity assessment. Grad-CAM, Otsu thresholding and GrabCut support explainable pseudo-segmentation. Mahalanobis screening and retrieval-augmented generation support unfamiliar-input handling and document-grounded advisory responses.

RESEARCH GAP & PROBLEM

Fragmented tools, connected needs

Germplasm identification requires specialist knowledge; land inspection is time-consuming; WCLWD can resemble other leaf disorders; management knowledge is dispersed across extension services and documents.

The project addresses limited application-level integration of these four coconut-management needs. Its contribution is the connected platform and component workflows, rather than the invention of the underlying algorithms.

Prior AI-based coconut research exists. The research gap is integration and context-specific implementation.

RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

What we set out to investigate

  1. Develop and evaluate few-shot coconut germplasm identification with visual explanations.
  2. Estimate aerial image occupancy and approximate additional planting capacity.
  3. Connect WCLWD classification, explanation, pseudo-segmentation and computational severity scoring.
  4. Combine disease classification, unfamiliar-input screening and bilingual knowledge-grounded advisory.
  5. Integrate all four modules through a common web platform and evaluate them independently.

01

COMPONENT DEEP DIVE

Germplasm identification

Dwarf, King Coconut and Tall germplasm are identified from field photographs collected at CRI, Bandirippuwa. A few-shot approach addresses limited labelled examples.

Method & outputs

RGB conversion → 224 × 224 resize → ImageNet normalization → EfficientNet-B0 → 512-dimensional embeddings → class prototypes → cosine similarity → class probabilities and Grad-CAM.

Data & evaluation

3-way, 8-shot, 5-query episodes; 341 training and 87 validation-pool images. Training used 40 epochs, with 40 training and 15 validation episodes per epoch.

Reported results

724 of 750 query instances were classified correctly across 50 evaluation episodes. Best episodic validation accuracy: 98.67%. Overall precision, recall and F1: 0.97.

Limits & next steps

Single-site, imbalanced dataset: Dwarf 118, King Coconut 67, Tall 243. Repeated episodic queries are not 750 unique external images. Multi-location and independent external testing are needed.

02

COMPONENT DEEP DIVE

Aerial land utilization

Aerial Palm/Tree detection is transformed into canopy occupancy, available image area and approximate additional planting positions.

Method & outputs

YOLO11n detections → Shapely circular buffers → Excess Green vegetation mask → elliptical constraints and morphological cleanup → Euclidean Distance Transform → local maxima → greedy spacing-constrained placement.

Data & evaluation

237 training and 42 validation images; 50 epochs, 640 × 640 inputs, batch size 8. Public Aerial Images of Palm Trees dataset.

Reported results

Precision 78.2%; recall 82.4%; mAP@50–95 48.9%. One demonstration image had 86 detections; refined utilization 23.81%, raw available area 76.19%; 172 candidates reduced to approximately 55 positions.

Limits & next steps

All area and spacing estimates are in pixel space. The demonstration uses a 148.2-pixel spacing proxy. No ground sampling distance, georeferencing, metric GIS or agronomic field validation is incorporated.

03

COMPONENT DEEP DIVE

WCLWD severity assessment

Classify Healthy Leaves, WCLWD Drying of Leaflets, WCLWD Flaccidity and WCLWD Yellowing, then visualize influential regions and estimate computational severity.

Method & outputs

Denoising and LAB/CLAHE enhancement → EfficientNet-B0 → Grad-CAM → image-specific Otsu thresholding → GrabCut and morphology → pseudo-mask → calibrated weighted Disease Severity Index.

Data & evaluation

Perceptual hashing and group-based splits reduce near-duplicate leakage. Approximate 70/15/15 split; minority Healthy-class oversampling is restricted to training.

Reported results

Weighted precision 98.80%, recall 98.79%, F1 98.77%. DSI = 0.20A + 0.20B + 0.20C + 0.15D + 0.25 × affected-area fraction, using calibrated descriptors. Mild ≤25; Moderate >25–50; Severe >50–75; Critical >75. Healthy predictions receive DSI 0.

Limits & next steps

Pseudo-masks are not manually annotated ground truth. Severity descriptors, weights and thresholds need expert agronomic validation. Classification accuracy does not establish severity-score accuracy.

04

COMPONENT DEEP DIVE

Disease identification & advisory

Identify CCI Caterpillars, Gray Leaf Spot, Healthy Leaves and Leaf Rot. Screen unfamiliar images and provide English/Sinhala management guidance through curated document retrieval.

Method & outputs

EfficientNet-B0 → 256-dimensional features → Mahalanobis OOD screen. Query → language detection → BGE embeddings → Pinecone top-5 retrieval → GPT-4o-mini through OpenRouter → bilingual response with conversational memory.

Data & evaluation

Original split: 3,364 training, 720 validation and 723 test images. Training balanced to 8,000 images. Knowledge base: 1,606 chunks, 900 characters with 150 overlap; BGE-large-en-v1.5 uses 1,024 dimensions.

Reported results

Test loss 0.3841. OOD threshold 594.53 at the 99th percentile; 0.97% of known in-distribution test images were flagged. RAG grounds responses in curated coconut disease documents.

Limits & next steps

No representative external OOD evaluation is documented. Retrieval quality, factuality and Sinhala translation require formal evaluation. The final advisory mechanism is RAG; crowdsourcing was an earlier concept.
